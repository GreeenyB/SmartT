SEGMENT 01
Fuel data becomes useful when it explains an operational event.

SEGMENT 02
That requires linking each reading to vehicle state, movement, time, and location.

SEGMENT 03
Smart T keeps that context intact from the vehicle to the operator.

SEGMENT 04
Signals are captured at the source, transmitted as telemetry, and processed for operational review.

SEGMENT 05
Operators can move from fleet-wide patterns to a specific vehicle, location, or event.

SEGMENT 06
Desktop and mobile views keep that workflow consistent across operations.

SEGMENT 07
The overview summarizes fleet status.

SEGMENT 08
Live Map adds location context, narrowing the fleet to vehicles in view.

SEGMENT 09
Selecting a vehicle reveals its route, fuel level, speed, ignition, and latest telemetry.

SEGMENT 10
Vehicle Detail brings fuel state, route, and telemetry together in one view.

SEGMENT 11
Over the last seven days, Fuel Analytics shows consumption, suspected loss, efficiency, and loss by depot.

SEGMENT 12
Reports summarize trends, costs, anomalies, depot comparisons, and exportable results.

SEGMENT 13
Now, incident handling begins.

SEGMENT 14
Suspected fuel loss rises to fifty-eight liters, triggering an alert.

SEGMENT 15
The incident view brings together vehicle identity, fuel levels, ignition, speed, and GPS location.

SEGMENT 16
The map and event-day fuel trend provide the supporting evidence.

SEGMENT 17
After review, the operator verifies the event.

SEGMENT 18
With the event verified, Smart T returns to the fleet overview.
